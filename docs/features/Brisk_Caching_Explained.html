Brisk — Caching concepts, in plain English

A quick reference for the Analytics terms: Origin offload, the P50 / P95 / P99 response-time percentiles, and the Origin shield role. Open this file in any browser.

1. Origin offload

Origin offload = how much of your traffic Brisk's edge servers answered on their own, so your real website server (your "origin") never had to. Higher % is better — faster pages for visitors, and less load + bandwidth cost on your server.

  • HIT = found in cache → served instantly, origin untouched.
  • MISS → origin = not in cache → had to fetch from your origin (the first request for any file is always a miss).
  • BYPASS = deliberately never cached (e.g. dynamic content, .m3u8 playlists).
  • Other = requests with no cache decision (404s, redirects, errors).
  • by request = share of cacheable requests served from cache. by bytes = share of data served from cache (and the MB that saved on your origin's bandwidth).

A cold cache shows low offload (lots of misses). As the same files get requested again they become HITs, and offload climbs on its own. A healthy CDN often sits at 80–95%.

2. Response time — P50 / P95 / P99

These are percentiles of how long the edge took to serve each request. Sort every request from fastest to slowest:

LabelTermMeans
TypicalP50 (median)Half of requests were faster than this — the typical visitor.
SlowP9595% were faster; only the slowest 5% were worse.
SlowestP9999% were faster; only the slowest 1% (worst tail) were worse.
AveragemeanPlain average — easily skewed by outliers, so percentiles are more honest.

Always P99 ≥ P95 ≥ P50. Percentiles matter more than the average because the average hides the painful tail. High latency usually means lots of cache misses proxying to a slow origin — it drops as the cache warms.

3. Origin shield

Origin shield = you designate one Brisk PoP as a middle cache layer between all the other edges and your origin server. Instead of every edge hitting your origin on a miss, they all go through the shield first — and the shield has its own cache, so most misses are answered there. Many edge misses collapse into a single origin fetch.

If you make NY the shield, does it also stay a public edge?

No. In Brisk, shield is a dedicated server role. The moment a server's role is shield, the control plane removes it from public DNS — visitors are never routed to it directly. So NY-as-shield stops being a user-facing PoP and becomes the hidden middle layer only. The role is server-wide (it affects every zone, not just one site). With a 3-edge fleet, that means you'd drop to 2 public edges (FRA + BLR) plus NY as the shield.
What you got right: yes — FRA and BLR, on a cache miss, send their request to NY (the shield) first; NY answers from its cache, and only on NY's own miss does it fetch from your origin once.

When it's worth it: a shield shines with many edges and a weak origin (e.g. shared-hosting WordPress) — collapsing everyone's misses into one fetch really protects the origin. For a small 3-box fleet, a shield is usually a separate dedicated box near your origin, or just left off.

Safety: if the shield is down, the other edges automatically fall back to the origin directly — "shield down" is never an outage. It's per-zone and off by default (enable it in a zone's Settings → Origin Shield).

One-line summary

Origin offload = how much your edges already shield your server. Origin shield = an extra cache layer that strengthens that protection by funneling all the misses through one place instead of hitting your origin from everywhere.
